# Initial label-blinded reading

This review uses only `requests.md`, `criteria.md`, and the initial A/B/C artifacts in this reader directory. Labels hide the methods; formatting may reveal them, so this is label-blinded reading, not assured blinding. C is assessed as its entrypoint **and** linked design. C was not assigned controls 2–4 here; their absence is not a defect. Withheld receipt links are not graded.

## Comparative result

All three case-1 sets provide a credible complete proposed architecture and an end-to-end repository first delivery. All choose the gateway for a reason supported by the packet, retain inbox/feedback on the same evaluator, carry restrictions from all consumed context rather than citations alone, and separate deterministic validation from real-model usefulness. None merely substitutes an intake prototype for the requested complete design. I found no demonstrated hard requirement contradiction. B has two material edge-case omissions to resolve before treating its recovery/novelty contract as complete; the other comments below are uncertainties, conservative choices, or usability costs, not requirement failures.

For a capable engineer joining this project, I provisionally prefer **A**: its central choices, information flow, first delivery and acceptance are readily explainable without relying on invented code organization. **C is at least as strong on substantive design and stronger on executable failure/race examples**; it could be preferable when the next reader is immediately assigning implementation tasks. Its extra specificity has real benefits as well as costs. B is a usable compact design with closely comparable coverage, but does not resolve stale evidence and send-time ongoing-work checks as explicitly as A/C. This is a task-specific preference from one packet, not evidence of general method superiority.

## Concrete strengths and findings

### A

- **Strength: distinct identities and freshness are connected to user behavior.** A/case-1:59–63 distinguishes delivery retries, repeated evaluations and equivalent suggestions, permits materially new follow-ups, and rechecks current work/source basis at approval and send. This directly explains how an implementer avoids both duplicate drafts and permanently silencing a resource.
- **Strength: restriction inheritance is operational.** A/case-1:51–55 covers unquoted model context, narrower destinations, renewed approval, revocation, logs and quiet hours. It does not confuse permission to retrieve with permission to disclose.
- **Strength: uncertain cost and crash behavior are explicit without a new platform.** A/case-1:67–75 fences commits, preserves newer generations and conservative charges, and makes limits durable across crashes.
- **Clarification, not established hard failure:** A/case-1:71 refers to the gateway's “existing admission budget” and a “configured gateway budget.” The packet establishes per-call reservation/admission but not an aggregate budget interface or its scope (requests:21). If an aggregate spend envelope is intended, name who configures/enforces it or mark that gateway capability as an assumption. Finite per-evaluation controls are already explicit at 73; the uncertainty is aggregate spend ownership, not lack of any cost bound.
- **Usability:** the first delivery table at 79–86 and the final walkthroughs at 92–98 are useful retrieval points. Some walkthroughs repeat the preceding contracts, but they make consequences assessable rather than adding procedural overhead.

### B

- **Strength: excellent compact source and restriction model.** B/case-1:36 distinguishes delivery and semantic evidence duplicates, including feedback identity. Lines 38–56 distinguish stable authority from hints and propagate all influencing context restrictions.
- **Strength: sensible quality qualification.** B/case-1:84–88 separates fake contract checks from a bounded pilot, samples quiet decisions, and requires an explicit continue/adjust/stop decision without inventing a numerical baseline.
- **B1 — Material missing freshness rule, not a demonstrated implementation bug.** B/case-1:36–42 treats each later resource revision as assessable evidence and records versions, but nowhere establishes whether a late notification for an older revision reads current mutable source state, or how opaque revisions are reconciled. Lines 46–48 explicitly obtain current task/conversation context but do not resolve current source revision. The supplied stable revision does not imply ordering. A new implementer could replay stale source content as a new reason to act; novelty/deduplication alone does not prevent that. Add an explicit current-source read/defer rule (A:33 and C/design:67 already do so).
- **B2 — Material missing timing for redundant-work revalidation.** B/case-1:48 and 74 compare ongoing work during assessment; 56–58 recheck authorization at approval/send and invalidate changed draft bases. But no passage requires rereading linked task state at approval/send. If work becomes handled after assessment without a new source delivery, the draft can remain authorized yet redundant. Require that check and a hold/invalidate outcome. A:63 and C/design:89 state it explicitly. This is a boundary-contract omission, not a missing runtime architecture.
- **Lesser recovery uncertainty:** B/case-1:76 requires retries to share limits but does not spell out unknown-usage reservations after crashes. Existing gateway settlement may already handle this; the source packet does not establish it. Do not infer either safe recovery or a proven leak. A:71 and C/design:103 make the conservative policy explicit.
- **Usability:** the next delivery is easy to find. Leaving individual/aggregate tool-output sizes adjustable at 76 is a reasonable implementation detail, not a failure to provide exact constants.

### C

- **Strength: failure examples are unusually executable.** C/case-1:151–164 tests generation/lease races and cross-audience competing work; 178–189 tests unquoted restricted context and final checks; 199–213 demonstrates provider acceptance followed by restart. These examples reduce real ambiguity, rather than merely promising “safe retries.”
- **Strength: context and budget boundaries are explicit.** C/case-1-design:83–89 separates principal, canonical relationships, scopes and derived output. Lines 101–111 define initial assembly in read bounds, durable admitted/uncertain call slots, quiet-hours-aware parking, idempotent delivery intent and continued reconciliation after revocation.
- **Ordinary conservative design choice:** C/case-1-design:69 permits only one pending suggestion per canonical work item across all audiences, holding further suggestions while delivery is pending. This is broader suppression than A's equivalent-action key (A:61–63). It may postpone a distinct useful blocker, but it deliberately avoids competing drafts and permits reevaluation afterward. The request does not require concurrent distinct suggestions; measure this tradeoff rather than call it a hard violation.
- **Clarification of claimed existing capabilities:** C/case-1-design:103 mentions the gateway's “existing recovery policy,” which is not supplied. C/case-1:189 calls enqueue “existing ... idempotent,” although the packet only establishes persistence and uncertain-send reconciliation. The design at 111 does require idempotent enqueue, and the whole set marks new contracts as proposals, so this is locally loose wording rather than an unsupported architecture. Present recovery/idempotency as requirements to verify or add, not established facts.
- **Usability cost with a clear benefit:** C/case-1:3 contains an execution-subskill requirement that line 13 then explains is inactive for this planning pass. The future implementer must interpret that process dependency even though the technical design requires no new runtime. Exact fixture APIs/file maps plus repeated red/run/implement/run/commit steps (60–235) help task assignment but constrain organization before source inspection and duplicate limits/state rules from the design. Keep the linked design canonical and allow existing repository conventions to replace proposed filenames/test APIs; line 39 already authorizes that adaptation. This is not a deduction based on length alone.
