## Material finding and useful differences

**C1 — Acknowledged limitation is not fully carried into the live-readiness decision.** The current requirements retain withdrawal “before approval or sending” (C/design:29; C/plan:23; update:5). C/design:124 correctly states that no supplied API makes remote deletion and external send atomic. However, the actual delivery demonstration accepts “no ... send after a locally observed pre-dispatch deletion” (C/design:136), and later qualification repeats that narrower standard (C/plan:269). C/plan:271 again explains the missing provider support but does not make its verification—or an explicitly agreed narrower guarantee—a prerequisite to live delivery. A builder could meet the stated qualification and enable approved sending while the unqualified deletion requirement remains unsettled. Preserve local demonstrations as useful progress, then add that explicit gate or recorded scope decision. This does **not** mean C conceals the race, lacks deletion handling, falsely claims tested atomicity, or cannot start independent implementation.

**A's advantage is explicit resolution criteria.** A:121–125 addresses both concurrent approval and provider acceptance, names bounded contract checks that could close the gap, and says a narrower guarantee requires explicit acceptance. A:100–101 ties that decision back to the delivery sequence. This is the strongest propagation from a capability limit to a reviewable readiness outcome. It does not establish that any proposed provider mechanism actually exists.

**B preserves the requirement with less detailed closure guidance.** B:72 retains the deletion guarantee, calls a host-observed boundary a requirement change, and B:115 makes pilot enablement conditional on resolution. Its discussion concentrates on refresh-to-provider acceptance; unlike A:121–123 it does not separately spell out the analogous remote-delete/concurrent-approval interval. The existing gate and shared approval refresh/generation checks make this a precision improvement, not a missing deletion lifecycle.

**C has concrete technical advantages for implementation.** C/design:120 and C/plan:95, 112 fence a fresh “present” read against a deletion arriving during that read, avoiding accidental restoration. C/design:110 and C/plan:166, 189 make estimate-only retries reuse the prepared decision without another model call. C/plan:199, 212 ensures later usage settlement updates the displayed actual/held amounts without changing the approved message. These details improve correctness and cost clarity; they are not merely extra headings or code volume.

**Cost approval policy differences are ordinary choices.** A:90 and B:80 allow unchanged content to send at an equal/lower estimate within the approved ceiling; C/design:114 requires renewed approval for any amount/unit change. The user required a visible estimate separate from investigation actuals, not one particular re-approval policy. Both approaches preserve individual approval. No method should lose credit merely for choosing one.

**No retrospective delivered-message recall is required.** All three distinguish unsent pending work from a provider-accepted send and preserve reconciliation after deletion (A:68; B:70; C/design:122, 132; C/plan:227–235). That is a necessary capability boundary, not abandonment of the pending-draft requirement.

## Limits

These are proposed contracts and planned tests, not verified code or provider behavior. No live source, model, approval, deletion, cost or delivery flow was run. The packet cannot establish whether external ordering support exists; the assessment evaluates how the plans expose and dispose of that uncertainty. The preference above concerns this update in one label-blinded comparison, not general method superiority. The initial findings were neither rewritten nor regraded.
