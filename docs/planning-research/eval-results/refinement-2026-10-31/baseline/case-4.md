# Investigating onboarding completion within 30 days

Investigate why people who start onboarding do or do not complete it **within 30 days**, retaining 7-day completion as a secondary view. The research should determine which explanation deserves the next validation: confusing setup, a mismatch between expectations and the product, or another barrier supported by the evidence. It should also allow the conclusion that the evidence is insufficient. We are not choosing a feature.

The later user update replaces the primary 7-day question with the 30-day question and explicitly includes expectation mismatch. Reminders remain a hypothesis, not the preferred intervention. Gamification is excluded. Use existing de-identified event data and interviews with consenting participants; do not add tracking or initiate unsolicited outreach.

**What the evidence establishes.** The [supplied case](../../../eval-fixtures/planning-refinement.md#4-early-onboarding-research) reports that 40 of 100 starters completed within 7 days last month: a reported 40% rate for that cohort and window. The arithmetic is **checked**; the underlying event data is not. The 30-day completion rate, time-to-completion distribution and reasons for non-completion are **unknown**. The six support messages are **observed in the supplied description** and suggest that confusing setup is worth investigating. They are not linked to the cohort and may concern existing users, so they neither explain the 60 non-completers at day 7 nor estimate how common confusion is among new users. There are no interviews or controlled comparisons yet.

**First establish the outcome and where it changes.** Ask the analyst to verify how the existing data defines a start and a completion, whether events are sufficiently reliable, and which starters have a full 30 days of observable follow-up. Report the eligible denominator and any exclusions. Do not count a person with only partial follow-up as a 30-day failure or relabel the 7-day result as a 30-day rate. If existing data cannot answer the primary question, state the gap and what remains learnable without new tracking.

For eligible starters, compare completion by day 7 and day 30 and inspect elapsed time, returns to onboarding and the last observed step. Use relevant existing attributes only where their meaning and completeness are understood. Distinguish people who complete after day 7 from those still incomplete at day 30. This can reveal where and when progress stops; event absence alone cannot establish confusion, disinterest or forgetfulness.

| Competing explanation | Evidence to seek next | Evidence that would weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| **Confusing setup** | Repeated attempts or concentrated exits at a setup step, alongside participant accounts of unclear instructions, requirements or what to do next. Ask participants to describe the point of difficulty in their own terms. | Participants understand the steps but intentionally stop because the result is not worth the effort; apparent setup concentration disappears after checking event quality. |
| **Expectations differ from what the product provides** | Participants describe a specific expected outcome and a point where the experience no longer seemed relevant. Check whether stopping follows that discovery rather than a procedural obstacle. | Participants still want the offered outcome and can explain it accurately, but a concrete setup obstacle prevents progress. |
| **Delay, competing priorities or forgetting** | People return and complete later without a changed setup; consenting participants describe continuing intent interrupted by timing or other demands. | People deliberately decided against the product, encountered an unresolved obstacle, or returned repeatedly without progressing. These patterns weaken a simple reminder hypothesis. |
| **Technical/access barrier or measurement error** | Existing events show failures or inconsistent sequences, and participant accounts identify a concrete failure; the analyst checks whether completion is recorded as intended. | Reliable completion records and accounts of deliberate abandonment or procedural confusion without evidence of a technical barrier. Absence of an error event is not conclusive if error coverage is unknown. |

Several explanations may coexist. The research should identify the conditions under which each matters rather than force every non-completer into one cause.

**Use interviews to interpret the patterns.** In parallel with the data check, the researcher can prepare neutral questions about the participant's original goal, expected result, actual experience, point of stopping and any later return. Interview consenting participants through an existing authorized route, seeking a useful contrast among early completers, later completers and people incomplete at 30 days where those groups are available. Do not recruit by unsolicited outreach. If no consenting participants or authorized recruitment route is available, interviews remain dependent on that access while event analysis continues.

Ask for concrete recollections before proposing explanations. Presenting reminders as the solution would bias the inquiry; instead investigate whether the person still wanted to finish, what prevented it, and what happened next. Interview accounts explain possible mechanisms but do not establish their population prevalence, especially when participation is selective. The six support messages may inform neutral questions, but must remain separate from cohort-linked evidence unless a valid link is actually available.

**The decision this should support.** Combine the analyst's cohort view with participant accounts to decide which barrier warrants the next focused validation and what evidence could disprove it. If most apparent 7-day losses complete by day 30, reconsider whether the problem is permanent loss or slower progress, and why that timing matters. If a specific step and confusion appear together, prioritize validating that barrier. If users intentionally stop because the offered outcome differs from expectations, investigate that mismatch. Reminders become a credible experiment candidate only if there is evidence of continued intent with a timing or return problem; even then, these observations do not prove reminders will help.

The research output should be a defensible 30-day cohort result, the secondary 7-day comparison, an explanation of the most informative event patterns, and a short synthesis of supporting and conflicting participant evidence. It should recommend the next question or validation, with remaining gaps. It need not contain a feature backlog, delivery dates or a rollout plan. If evidence conflicts or the consenting sample is too narrow, the next step is a discriminating observation rather than a premature feature choice.

No clarification is needed to begin this direction. The consequential unknowns are data adequacy and participant access, which the permitted first checks can establish. Outcomes are **pending — no event analysis or interviews have been performed**.
